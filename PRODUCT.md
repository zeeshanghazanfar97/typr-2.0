# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Typr is for people who dictate into existing desktop apps and want speech input to feel faster than opening a separate tool. They are usually mid-flow in a browser, editor, chat app, or document, and the interface should stay out of the way until they need to record or adjust a transform.

## Product Purpose

Typr captures audio from a global hotkey or compact floating control, transcribes it locally or through Groq, optionally polishes the transcript, and pastes the result into the focused application. Success is measured by fast capture, low interruption, reliable fallback behavior, and a persistent control that feels native on macOS.

## Brand Personality

Quiet, precise, fluid. The product should feel like a small desktop instrument: present when needed, nearly invisible otherwise.

## Anti-references

Avoid dashboard-like chrome, oversized settings windows as the primary experience, heavy color gradients, decorative panels, and novelty controls that make dictation feel slower than typing.

## Design Principles

- The floating control is the product surface; settings support it.
- Keep the default state tiny, calm, and predictable.
- Make transforms obvious only when the user is near the control.
- Favor native-feeling density and direct manipulation over explanatory copy.
- Fallbacks should be silent and reliable.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Use standard system fonts, visible focus states, large enough pointer targets for the floating controls, and reduced-motion-friendly state transitions. Maintain keyboard and global-hotkey access for users who do not want to rely on the pointer.
